What we were looking for

Three beds for a future sound sanctuary or clock sanctuary pack:

  1. Underground cave water: drips, trickles, flowing underground streams.
  2. Struck stalactite or stalagmite tones — speleothem / lithophone percussion, like tapping cave stone with a wooden tool.
  3. Cave ambience, drone, or room tone.

Preferred hosts: Freesound, Pixabay, BigSoundBank. Aim: about three to five solid candidates per category.

Ahsan admires a sound therapist named Stefan who records in French Ice Age caves and taps stalagmites with a wooden tool. This report seeks freely licensed equivalents only. It does not source, link, or recommend Stefan’s recordings as usable app audio.

Verified background only: the Great Stalacpipe Organ in Luray Caverns, Virginia, is a real lithophone that strikes tuned stalactites with rubber-tipped plungers. See the Luray Caverns Great Stalacpipe Organ page and the Great Stalacpipe Organ Wikipedia article. That is context, not a free sample library.

Category 2 — struck stalactite and stalagmite tones

Honesty: Genuine Creative Commons recordings of wooden-tool or mallet taps on cave stalactites or stalagmites are scarce to nearly absent on Freesound, Pixabay and BigSoundBank (checked 9 November 2026). Freesound searches for “stalactite” mostly return water dripping from formations. A “lithophone” search returned no useful hits. The candidates below are labelled substitutes — ringing rocks, mallet on granite, glassy stone chime, rock on rock — not French Ice Age cave taps.

Top substitute pick: ringing rocks.wav by tim.kahn — real resonant struck stone with a metallic ring.

Context only — not recommended for shipping

  • Smartphone recording of the Great Stalacpipe Organ by designerschoice — page shows CC BY 4.0, about 1:27. Tourist organ performance, wrong character for wooden-tool meditation taps; musical performance rights may sit outside a simple upload licence. Listed for honesty only.

Labelled substitutes (verified)

Category 3 — cave ambience and drone

Top pick: INT Cave Room tone from a cave in Kent — real room tone, CC0. Pair it with a drip bed rather than relying on designed horror drones alone.

  1. INT Cave Room tone.wav by deleted_user_2104797.

    • URL: Freesound page for INT Cave Room tone
    • Licence: CC0 1.0
    • Duration: about 1:54, Wave, mono
    • Notes: Room tone for a cave in Kent, England. Best quiet real bed. Listen for distant hum before shipping.
    • Verified: 9 November 2026

  2. Dark Cave Drone by Kinoton.

    • URL: Freesound page for Dark Cave Drone
    • Licence: CC0 1.0 on the sound page, plus Kinoton profile Terms of Use
    • Duration: about 4:17
    • Notes: Designed from thunder-drum layers, not a field cave mic. Kinoton allows commercial use and editing, but forbids selling or dumping the raw files as stock. Fine to embed in the app; do not offer the raw WAV as a download pack.
    • Verified: 9 November 2026

  3. Dark Dungeon Ambience by Kinoton.

    • URL: Freesound page for Dark Dungeon Ambience
    • Licence: CC0 1.0 + same Kinoton terms
    • Duration: about 3:00
    • Notes: Drone plus ceiling drips and air howl. If you also load a separate drip bed, turn one drip layer down.
    • Verified: 9 November 2026

  4. BigSoundBank Cave #1 and Cave #2 by Joseph SARDIN.

    • URLs: Cave #1; Cave #2

Top picks and first mix

  • Cave water top pick: nomadas Ojo Guareña drips — real karst, CC0, clean stereo drips.
  • Struck speleothem top pick: none genuine under CC. Closest substitute: tim.kahn ringing rocks, clearly labelled.
  • Cave ambience top pick: INT Cave Room tone (Kent) — real quiet room tone, CC0.

Suggested first listening mix for VoiceOver:

  1. Room tone: INT Cave Room tone.
  2. Water: nomadas Ojo Guareña (or Benboncan mine drips for a longer bed).
  3. Optional sparse taps: sliced ringing-rocks or granite-mallet hits, spoken as stone percussion substitutes, not Stefan-style cave taps.
