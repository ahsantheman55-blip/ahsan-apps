Vision, target player and status

Latest confirmed: an accessible chess board Ahsan plays on, where every move is tested against the standard rules of chess. The app is a test teacher rather than a pure engine. It contains an engine, but the engine is advisory: it steps in when Ahsan is totally lost, not constantly.

Latest confirmed: the target player is a club-level player whose strength swings considerably. On a real board he plays at club level; playing fast or in his head, he plays like an introductory novice. Memory is not very good. The app should serve that gap and help the mental-play weakness. This is personal context for design, not a medical diagnosis.

Status: this brief records intent and proposals. An earlier chess coach increment exists as a separate project; this document does not describe its status, promise a roadmap or decide how the two relate. Labels used: Latest confirmed (from the newest instructions), Earlier requested (from previous voice notes, compatible but not re-confirmed), and Proposed (design suggestions, not requirements).

Guiding principles

Latest confirmed: training, not tournament play. Advice is triggered by a tap, never pushed automatically. Insight should be deep: tactical, strategic and positional, exposing plan errors and failures to convert a winning position.

Earlier requested: be direct and human, grounded in real played games rather than complicated textbook orthodoxy; build incrementally with a working core first.

Proposed: never pretend to know more than the analysis supports. The app cannot know Ahsan’s intentions; it can only infer a likely plan from the moves played and should say so.

Accessible board and legal moves

Latest confirmed: the board enforces standard legality whenever a piece moves.

Proposed for a totally blind VoiceOver user: every square, piece and move is reachable and announced by name, for example “White knight, f3”. Move entry by typed or dictated text such as “knight to f three” as well as square-by-square selection. Each move confirms what moved, any capture, check, castling, en passant or promotion, and whose turn follows. Board state, move history and the last move can be reread at any time.

Reconciliation with the open legality decision: the rule checker always knows what is legal. An illegal attempt must never silently enter or corrupt the recorded legal game. Whether an illegal attempt is simply rejected with an explanation, or explored in a separate hypothetical mode as a teaching moment, is not decided (see Open decisions).

Tap-only advice and feedback boundaries

Latest confirmed: updates are tap-only. Ahsan explicitly chose tap-triggered updates over automatic ones because he is requesting many features. There are no automatic recaps, unprompted prompts or live running commentary.

Proposed distinction: essential accessibility feedback is not coaching. Announcing the move just made, a rejected move, check or game end is action feedback and should always happen. Coaching, evaluation and recaps happen only when Ahsan taps for them.

Latest confirmed: VoiceOver updates “at a certain time” are desired, details to be decided. This brief invents no schedule, timer or trigger for them.

Recap of the latest three or four major motifs

Latest confirmed: on request, the app recaps the latest three or four major tactical motifs sweeping around the board.

Proposed: each recap item names the motif, the pieces and squares involved, the move that created it, and whether it is still live. Include a short recent-move context so Ahsan remembers what both sides were attempting.

Illustrative wording only, not from a real position: “A pin: your knight on f6 is pinned to your queen by the bishop on g5. A fork threat: their knight could reach c7, attacking king’s rook and rook. A weak back rank: your king has no escape square, so a rook check on the eighth rank deserves respect.”

Proposed: label a recognised motif or evaluated pattern differently from a proven forced line. A motif is a pattern worth noticing; a forced line is only claimed when the analysis has actually verified it.

Information buttons

Latest confirmed: information buttons are not merely informational. They reveal deep strategic errors, or show that although Ahsan has been winning he does not know how to face the opponent off.

Earlier requested, organised here as compatible candidates: What The Heck (now specified in its own section below). Where Is Everything or I’m Lost, which reads the board with sub-options such as positional, piece placement, early-game fog and an individual piece. Attack, covering both sides’ attacks. Defence, covering both sides’ defences. Initiative, saying who has it with a short recap of recent moves. Checkmating prospects and King danger: how close Ahsan is to checkmating, and how much danger his own king is in. The earlier wording was “capturing the king”; in real chess the king is never captured, so the button speaks of checkmate. Position: strengths, weaknesses, pawn structure, piece placement and king safety.

Proposed: no mate-in-four or mate-in-five claims, candidate moves or forced lines unless validated by analysis. Candidates are offered, not played through; Ahsan must still play the moves.

Board description modes — screen-reader first

Latest confirmed (newest request): two board description modes for when Ahsan gets lost. The regular board description remains the default.

Latest confirmed: once the game is in motion, a tap requests an alternative whole-board description ordered from the most attacked or most active pieces first, through the entire piece list, down to the most dormant or least useful. It gives a strategic read without needing to visualise squares. Tap-only: no automatic updates and no new quiz.

Proposed: being attacked (vulnerable) and being active (productive) are not the same thing; the description should make clear which applies. Every piece of both sides is included, each identified by colour, piece and square, so nothing is omitted however dormant. Each entry carries a short label saying why it sits where it does. No scoring method is set here.

Proposed wording boundary: a quiet defender can be crucial, so the app should not call a piece useless as an objective certainty. Neutral terms such as “least active” are used, delivered within the blunt coaching style.

Open questions, not resolved: how activity is measured — attacks received, attacks made, legal mobility, centre control, role, or a blend, and with what weighting; whether urgent threats are prioritised over productivity; whether both sides are interleaved or grouped; how ties, pawns and kings are handled; and how to navigate a long read, for example by section or piece.

What The Heck — tap-only emergency button

Latest confirmed: a tap-requested emergency button whose response depends on Ahsan’s state, delivered in the sermon-like didactic character. It does not soften merely because he has been battered all game.

Playing well: a conversion prompt in the spirit of “why haven’t I captured the enemy king by now?” In real chess that means why he has not yet checkmated or converted the advantage; it should name what is holding the conversion back.

Playing badly: a brutally honest assessment. It states the inferred previous plan (inferred, never claimed as known), how material and position deteriorated, then a sensible proactive plan rather than mere reaction to the opponent’s moves. Its purpose is to get Ahsan out of defeatism, not to criticise only.

Desired style, quoted from Ahsan: “you had a plan, you were playing okay, but now you’re losing material and position, and let’s be honest, you’re going to lose.” Proposed: the app distinguishes a practically losing evaluation from a proven loss, and only states a certain result when a forced result is proven. Candidate moves carry their uncertainty.

Almost or totally lost: two paths are offered — resign, or be brave and fight back with a concrete resistance plan built from legal candidate moves. The app never resigns automatically and never guarantees that fighting back will save the game.

Compare button — screen-reader first

Latest confirmed: a tap-requested Compare button with a submenu of five comparisons: pawn structure, pawn placement, king safety, piece activity and initiative. All five are required. Each is spoken and screen-reader friendly.

Proposed: although described as side by side, nothing depends on spatial or tabular layout. Each comparison is read sequentially: your side, then the opponent’s side, then the key difference and its actionable consequence.

Illustrative distinction, not formal definitions or final engine weights: pawn structure covers the pattern — connected, isolated or doubled pawns, chains, islands and passed pawns. Pawn placement covers exact squares — how far pawns have advanced, blockades, and which pieces support or restrain them.

Mental play and memory support

Latest confirmed: help the gap between board play and fast or mental play.

Proposed: externalise the board state so nothing must be held in memory: reread the position by piece, by square or by rank; reread the full move history or only the last few moves; restore an exploration start point. Earlier requested: optional exploration of lines with a saved starting point to return to before a dangerous line. Exploration must stay separate from the real game.

Tone and coaching voice

Latest confirmed: sermon-like and didactic, in intelligent English, blunt in the spirit of “Why are you playing crap chess? Why are you asking me?” No puzzle cheerleading. Useful both for a weaker player and for a strong player who cannot face the opponent off.

Earlier requested: an intellectual, slightly wordy, Shakespearean flavour; it tells rather than quizzes; no compliments for good play, just quiet confirmation nothing is broken.

Proposed: the bluntness criticises decisions and always carries a useful explanation. No random insults and no hollow compliments.

Engine and advisory boundaries

Latest confirmed: the engine advises, mainly when Ahsan is totally lost.

Proposed: every piece of advice should say what it rests on — an evaluated position, a recognised motif or a proven forced line — and admit analysis depth limits. Advice must never invent real games, players or results. Historic analogies may only cite verified games.

Earlier scope and phase options — proposal

Earlier requested scope, not required for a first release: a verified historic game library with analogies to the current opening or position; endgame instruction with classic positions such as Lucena and Philidor, explained in the sermon voice; and a post-game review that walks the game back move by move and saves happy training moments.

Proposed phases, not a promise: first the accessible board, legality, reread and history, and tap-only recap; then the information buttons; then endgames, historic games and post-game review.

Open decisions

These four are preserved exactly as unresolved and are not settled here.

One: should the sermon-like voice stay in character however frustrated Ahsan gets, or soften on request?

Two: VoiceOver timing details for updates “at a certain time”.

Three: engine strength and settings, and how much the advisory layer intervenes versus letting Ahsan struggle.

Four: how legality testing interacts with advice — block illegal moves, or allow them as teaching moments, for example in a separate hypothetical mode.

Nuance, not a resolution: the latest instruction sets the default that What The Heck does not soften automatically after setbacks. That does not settle question one, whether the voice should soften when Ahsan explicitly asks.

Feature list may be incomplete: Ahsan mentioned “a few other things that you might remember that I don’t”. He may add more; nothing further is invented here.

Secondary, if needed: how the threshold for “totally lost” is judged; and how this design relates to the existing chess coach increment.

Acceptance criteria

Rules, deterministic: legal and illegal moves for every piece; castling both sides including through-check and moved-piece restrictions; en passant only immediately; promotion to each piece; check, checkmate, stalemate and draw rules; illegal attempts never alter the recorded game.

Accessibility: full play with VoiceOver on an iPhone and a desktop browser, keyboard only, no visual dependency; every move and rejection announced; board and history rereadable.

Board description: the regular description is the default; the alternative ranked mode appears only on tap; it lists every piece of both sides with colour, piece and square; each position in the ranking has a stated reason, with the ranking method still to be decided.

What The Heck: on tap, gives a state-appropriate response — conversion, recovery plan, or a choice between resigning and fighting back — with legal candidate moves and their uncertainty stated; no automatic resignation and no certainty claims without a proven forced result.

Compare: available only on request; all five comparisons present; each names your side and the opponent’s explicitly and gives reasons.

Tap-only: no coaching, recap or prompt appears without a tap; essential action feedback still announced.

Advice grounding: motifs in recaps actually exist on the board; forced lines and mate claims verified; inferred plans labelled as inferred; historic games verified; the tone critiques decisions with an explanation.
