Ahsan’s Interest Camera — Design Brief

Research checked 8 November 2026 (Europe/London). Separate project; design only.

Purpose and feasibility

A separate project from the word game: a mobile JavaScript app that describes photographed subjects and explains whether they match interests Ahsan has explicitly chosen. Feasible as a foreground, point-and-shoot tool using Safari on iPhone and a supported Android browser, with an HTTPS backend for image analysis. “Interesting” means matching saved preferences, not reading the user’s mind. This is a design brief, not a built or device-tested app.

Important iPhone home-screen caveat

Start in a normal, top-level Safari tab. Home-screen web apps can access cameras, but WebKit has documented device- and OS-dependent failures in standalone mode. Historical reports include later fixes; they do not prove every current PWA is broken. Permission persistence also varies. Test the actual iPhone, iOS version and VoiceOver configuration before offering installation. Safari itself still needs lifecycle testing: locking, switching apps and returning can interrupt capture.

The first, small increment

One page with Interests, Take photo, Analyse, Read result, Repeat and Stop controls. Begin with a labelled file input accepting images and requesting the rear camera. Its capture attribute is a hint: the operating system may offer a camera or photo library rather than opening the camera directly. Explicitly confirm analysis before uploading. No continuous scanning, background push notifications or installed-PWA requirement initially. Add a live preview only after the single-photo experience works reliably.

Core loop and accessible feedback

Choose interests and their specificity: “birds generally” differs from “identify bird species”; architecture can mean all buildings or particular styles. Capture a photo, confirm upload, announce “Analysing”, then return what appears visible, which preference it matches, why, and any uncertainty. Distinguish “no selected interest detected” from “image too unclear to judge”. Offer another photograph with short, cautious aiming advice, such as holding steady or changing the angle—not instructions to move into danger.

Use semantic headings, native labelled controls, predictable focus and a polite live region. Never rely solely on highlights, colour or a preview. Optional speech must not talk over VoiceOver; provide replay, stop, speech rate and volume settings. Announce one concise result per scan, not a stream of interruptions. Settings include editable interests, broad-versus-specific matching, verbosity, speech choice and whether results are retained. No account is needed merely to remember preferences locally.

Camera engineering: corrections to the rough research

Live capture uses getUserMedia over HTTPS with user permission. Permission is required, but a fresh prompt every session is not universal: browser settings and prior grants matter. Prefer an ideal rear-facing camera request; an exact environment requirement can reject when unavailable. Offer camera switching and understandable denial/unavailable messages. Embedded previews need the host’s camera Permissions Policy; opening directly in Safari avoids that extra dependency.

The blanket claim that Safari lacks ImageCapture is now outdated. MDN’s current compatibility data records Safari support for takePhoto from 18.4 and grabFrame from 26. Feature-detect individual methods and retain a video-to-canvas fallback. Prefer canvas toBlob for upload rather than memory-heavy base64 toDataURL. Camera controls depend on advertised track capabilities and hardware; do not promise them or claim none exist. Stop tracks explicitly when finished; do not rely on background camera operation.

Vision backend and matching

Provisionally use a general multimodal vision API behind our own server. OpenAI’s documented image-input API is one candidate, not a committed provider or model. Benchmark realistic photos before choosing on accuracy, latency, cost and retention terms. Ask for structured observations, uncertainty, image-quality issues and relevant categories, then compare those with an explicit interest list and category hierarchy. Explain the match in ordinary language. Model confidence is not automatically a calibrated probability.

Specialist bird recognition or OCR can be researched later if general vision is inadequate. Number-plate reading should be an optional, explicitly requested text task, not owner identification or vehicle tracking. Text visible inside images is untrusted data, never instructions to the app; model results must not trigger tools or external actions.

Risks, privacy and release gate

Distant birds may occupy too few pixels for species identification. Motion blur, bright skies, occlusion and small text can defeat recognition; invented certainty is unacceptable. Uploads need connectivity, consume data and incur backend costs; latency and battery use grow with repeated scans. Announce network failures and provide cancellation. No offline recognition is promised.

Keep provider credentials exclusively server-side. Apply backend authentication or session controls, upload-size limits, rate limits and spending caps. Explain where images go before upload; minimise incidental faces and location metadata. Default to no saved image history, delete temporary app copies promptly, and distinguish that from the provider’s retention policy, which must be checked before launch. Give users control over retained results. This is not navigation, hazard detection or a safety aid.

Release only after physical iPhone/VoiceOver and Android testing covers capture, permission refusal, uncertainty, upload failure, speech conflicts and app switching. No service account, paid commitment, public hosting or application build has been undertaken.
