Ahsan’s compact chess opening library

Seven illustrative, mainstream lines. Each contains six full moves: 42 full moves total, or 84 individual moves (plies). The SAN and expanded descriptions below are two representations of the same moves, not extra variations.

These lines are not forced, and are not the only good or best moves. Opponents can deviate at any point. Use the plans as guides, not a script; check threats before following a plan.

How to use: choose one opening, replay its six moves for both sides, then practise the resulting position. After each opponent move, ask: what changed, what is threatened, and is my intended pawn break still safe?

SAN means Standard Algebraic Notation. The expanded move lists spell out the moving piece and its starting and finishing squares. “Captures” means an enemy piece is taken. Square names use file letters a to h and rank numbers 1 to 8.

Italian Game

SAN: 1. e4 e5 2. Nf3 Nc6 3. Bc4 Bc5 4. c3 Nf6 5. d3 d6 6. O-O O-O

Expanded moves

  1. White pawn e2 to e4. Black pawn e7 to e5.
  2. White knight g1 to f3. Black knight b8 to c6.
  3. White bishop f1 to c4. Black bishop f8 to c5.
  4. White pawn c2 to c3. Black knight g8 to f6.
  5. White pawn d2 to d3. Black pawn d7 to d6.
  6. White castles king-side: king e1 to g1, rook h1 to f1. Black castles king-side: king e8 to g8, rook h8 to f8.

Middlegame guide

White: develop the queen-side knight and bishop; prepare a central expansion while keeping pressure on f7.

Black: finish development, contest the centre and avoid letting White build up unchallenged.

Pawn breaks: White d4 after preparation; Black d5 when adequately supported.

Pitfall: an early attack on f7 can waste time or sacrifice material without enough support.

Ruy Lopez

SAN: 1. e4 e5 2. Nf3 Nc6 3. Bb5 a6 4. Ba4 Nf6 5. O-O Be7 6. Re1 b5

Expanded moves

  1. White pawn e2 to e4. Black pawn e7 to e5.
  2. White knight g1 to f3. Black knight b8 to c6.
  3. White bishop f1 to b5. Black pawn a7 to a6.
  4. White bishop b5 to a4. Black knight g8 to f6.
  5. White castles king-side: king e1 to g1, rook h1 to f1. Black bishop f8 to e7.
  6. White rook f1 to e1. Black pawn b7 to b5.

Middlegame guide

White: retreat the attacked bishop to b3 next, then prepare c3 and d4; a knight can later manoeuvre through d2 and f1.

Black: develop the bishop from c8 and castle, maintaining the e5 strongpoint and seeking central counterplay.

Pawn breaks: White d4, usually supported by c3; Black d5 after preparation.

Pitfall: White cannot simply win the e5 pawn by exchanging on c6 and taking it; Black can often recover material with a queen attack.

Sicilian Defence — Najdorf setup

SAN: 1. e4 c5 2. Nf3 d6 3. d4 cxd4 4. Nxd4 Nf6 5. Nc3 a6 6. Be2 e5

Expanded moves

  1. White pawn e2 to e4. Black pawn c7 to c5.
  2. White knight g1 to f3. Black pawn d7 to d6.
  3. White pawn d2 to d4. Black pawn c5 captures on d4.
  4. White knight f3 captures on d4. Black knight g8 to f6.
  5. White knight b1 to c3. Black pawn a7 to a6.
  6. White bishop f1 to e2. Black pawn e7 to e5.

Middlegame guide

White: move the attacked knight from d4, often to b3, then castle and develop; use the d5 outpost when possible.

Black: develop the bishops, castle and seek queen-side space and activity on the half-open c-file.

Pawn breaks: Black b5 for queen-side space and d5 for central liberation; White f4 when development and king safety allow.

Pitfall: after Black plays e5, leaving the knight on d4 without a concrete reason can lose it to the e-pawn.

Queen’s Gambit — Declined

SAN: 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e6 3. Nc3 Nf6 4. Bg5 Be7 5. e3 O-O 6. Nf3 h6

Expanded moves

  1. White pawn d2 to d4. Black pawn d7 to d5.
  2. White pawn c2 to c4. Black pawn e7 to e6.
  3. White knight b1 to c3. Black knight g8 to f6.
  4. White bishop c1 to g5. Black bishop f8 to e7.
  5. White pawn e2 to e3. Black castles king-side: king e8 to g8, rook h8 to f8.
  6. White knight g1 to f3. Black pawn h7 to h6.

Middlegame guide

White: resolve the bishop attack, develop the f1 bishop and castle; build pressure on the centre and use the c-file if it opens.

Black: develop the c8 bishop, often after a queen-side pawn exchange or b6, and challenge White’s centre.

Pawn breaks: Black c5 is the main freeing break; White e4 needs preparation.

Pitfall: exchanging on d5 automatically can release central tension without gaining anything; first decide which pawn structure you want.

London System

SAN: 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. Bf4 e6 4. e3 c5 5. c3 Nc6 6. Nbd2 Bd6

Expanded moves

  1. White pawn d2 to d4. Black pawn d7 to d5.
  2. White knight g1 to f3. Black knight g8 to f6.
  3. White bishop c1 to f4. Black pawn e7 to e6.
  4. White pawn e2 to e3. Black pawn c7 to c5.
  5. White pawn c2 to c3. Black knight b8 to c6.
  6. White knight b1 to d2. Black bishop f8 to d6.

Middlegame guide

White: develop the f1 bishop, castle and consider a knight on e5; respond thoughtfully to the bishop challenge on d6.

Black: challenge d4, finish development and look for pressure on b2 or an eventual central expansion.

Pawn breaks: White e4 after preparation; Black has already played c5 and can later seek e5.

Pitfall: repeating the same setup regardless of Black’s moves can overlook pressure on b2 or a tactical threat in the centre.

Caro-Kann Defence — Classical

SAN: 1. e4 c6 2. d4 d5 3. Nc3 dxe4 4. Nxe4 Bf5 5. Ng3 Bg6 6. h4 h6

Expanded moves

  1. White pawn e2 to e4. Black pawn c7 to c6.
  2. White pawn d2 to d4. Black pawn d7 to d5.
  3. White knight b1 to c3. Black pawn d5 captures on e4.
  4. White knight c3 captures on e4. Black bishop c8 to f5.
  5. White knight e4 to g3. Black bishop f5 to g6.
  6. White pawn h2 to h4. Black pawn h7 to h6.

Middlegame guide

White: gain king-side space, develop the bishops and choose a safe home for the king; long castling is one possible plan.

Black: play e6, develop the remaining pieces and arrange central counterplay without trapping the light-squared bishop.

Pawn breaks: Black c5 challenges d4; White h5 gains space but should fit the king-safety plan.

Pitfall: Black’s light-squared bishop can be trapped by advancing pawns if its retreat squares are neglected.

French Defence — Advance

SAN: 1. e4 e6 2. d4 d5 3. e5 c5 4. c3 Nc6 5. Nf3 Qb6 6. a3 Nh6

Expanded moves

  1. White pawn e2 to e4. Black pawn e7 to e6.
  2. White pawn d2 to d4. Black pawn d7 to d5.
  3. White pawn e4 to e5. Black pawn c7 to c5.
  4. White pawn c2 to c3. Black knight b8 to c6.
  5. White knight g1 to f3. Black queen d8 to b6.
  6. White pawn a2 to a3. Black knight g8 to h6.

Middlegame guide

White: support d4, develop the bishops and castle; b4 can gain queen-side space, while the e5 pawn gives king-side prospects.

Black: concentrate on d4, bring the knight towards f5 and find a useful role for the restricted c8 bishop.

Pawn breaks: Black f6 attacks the head of White’s pawn chain; c5 has already attacked its base. White b4 is a possible space-gaining break.

Pitfall: White’s d4 pawn is under real pressure; a slow wing attack can allow the centre to collapse.
