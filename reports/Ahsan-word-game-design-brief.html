Product vision and status

Confirmed: words are the spine of this game. Everything else orbits a target word. The eventual game can be complicated, drawing on music theory, chess patterns, chemistry, history and any other discipline the player knows a little about. Those connections should illuminate the word, not reduce it to an incidental password for an unrelated quiz.

This is not a vocabulary test. Its purpose is discovery rather than recall alone. Ahsan loves words but does not consider himself good at word games; the experience should deepen that love rather than expose gaps in memorised knowledge. Complexity is a future ambition, not a requirement for the first build.

This document preserves the supplied concept. Recommendations below are design suggestions, not additional confirmed requirements. Open decisions remain explicitly unsettled. This is a reference brief, not an app implementation.

Tone, philosophy and boundaries

Confirmed: reward depth and cleverness fairly, while keeping the exact answer worth pursuing. Words should feel alive. Relevant thinking deserves recognition, but wit must never replace knowledge.

Recommended: explain what the player understood, what they missed and why their award follows. Avoid random bonuses, manipulative praise and consolation points unrelated to the answer. Do not turn play into an examination or require expertise across every discipline.

Timers, hints, multiplayer, progression systems and an AI judge are not requirements. A chemistry clue must be answerable within its stated context: unrelated musical or historical knowledge must not secretly be necessary for maximum credit.

A simple round loop — recommended

Present one clearly stated clue centred on one target word. Identify the relevant context without disclosing the answer. Let the player submit a word and optionally a short explanation or witty interpretation; do not make every turn a compulsory essay.

Assess the answer against the prepared rubric. Reveal the target, announce the award band and explain the decision. Offer one useful connection that brings the word alive. Allow the player to reread the clue, their answer and the explanation before deliberately continuing.

Make the award rules available before play. This proposed loop is a starting point, not a settled interface specification.

Confirmed scoring principles

Exact answer: maximum points. The player should always aim for the exact word. A perfect answer in a chemistry context earns the maximum without an additional cross-disciplinary flourish.

On-topic but not exact: partial credit recognises relevant reasoning, not memory alone. Merely naming another word from the same subject should not automatically demonstrate understanding.

Exceptional relevant wit: a wrong word can recover a substantial award, nearly as much as a correct answer, but never equal to or greater than the exact-answer maximum. The insight must orbit the word or its stated context. Miss both the word and the insight, and the award is zero.

Recommended ordinal bands: maximum for an accepted exact answer; near-maximum for exceptional relevant wit; partial for substantive on-topic reasoning; zero for neither. No numerical values have been agreed. Partial reasoning and wit should produce one adjudicated award, not independent bonuses that stack. No wrong answer can accumulate its way to maximum. An exact answer already receives maximum, with no extra wit bonus.

A high-bar wit rubric — recommended

Ask whether the response is relevant, precise, substantive and unexpectedly illuminating. It should connect to this word or context, demonstrate understanding rather than generic humour, and reveal a genuinely clever angle.

Exceptional compensation should require all four qualities. Explain which qualities the response demonstrates instead of merely declaring it funny. A pun may qualify if it carries real insight; puns are not categorically forbidden. A bare pun, stock joke or amusing digression is insufficient.

Use prepared benchmark examples and apply the same standards across rounds. The near-maximum ceiling recognises extraordinary thinking without creating an easier substitute for finding the exact answer. Relevance alone is not enough for this award.

Invented worked example: catalyst

This example illustrates the concept; it is not final game content. Clue: “In chemistry, what word describes a substance that increases a reaction’s rate without being consumed overall?” Intended target: catalyst.

Exact answer: “Catalyst.” Maximum award. A longer explanation should not be necessary.

On-topic response: “Accelerator — something that makes the reaction faster.” This demonstrates part of the intended idea, but omits the non-consumption distinction. Partial credit may be appropriate if the clue and accepted-answer policy clearly distinguish this from an accepted alternative. Review legitimate scientific terminology before judging it wrong.

Potential exceptional wit: “A chemical conductor: it gets the orchestra reacting without becoming part of the tune.” The word is wrong, but the analogy connects facilitation and non-consumption to the clue. Substantial compensation is possible if it meets the calibrated high bar, not guaranteed merely because it sounds clever.

Off-topic response: “Banana — because chemistry needs a laugh.” Neither the word nor a relevant insight lands: zero.

Future connections could involve musical conductors, chess moves that unlock activity or historical catalysts. These remain analogies, not literal chemical definitions or hidden requirements for this chemistry round.

Fairness and ambiguity — recommended

Separate genuinely wrong words from valid alternatives. A synonym or defensible interpretation is not a mistake rescued by wit. If a clue reasonably admits multiple answers, accept or review them, revise the clue, or withdraw the round rather than penalise the player. Exact-answer and synonym policy remains open.

Normalise harmless capitalisation and punctuation. Consider dialect, spelling variants, typographical slips and dictation errors before deciding that the player misunderstood the word. Provide a correction or review route without revealing answers prematurely. Accent or speech-recognition failure should not become a knowledge penalty.

Explain uncertain decisions honestly. Consistent reasoning should receive consistent treatment, including when one player writes more elegantly than another.

Accessibility from the first increment — recommended

Design for a blind VoiceOver user from the outset: semantic headings, labelled controls, logical reading and focus order, and full keyboard operation. Every clue, answer and explanation must be available as text. Never rely on colour, imagery or audio alone.

Announce the award and its reason once without unnecessary interruption, then keep both available to revisit. Avoid disappearing feedback and mandatory time pressure. Optional spoken output needs an equivalent text experience.

A simple, accessible first build protects both builder and player from discouragement. These are design safeguards, not a claim of completed device testing.

First increment, content records and acceptance criteria — recommended

Start with a modest curated question set, one round loop and clear feedback. Include reviewed examples of every award band before expanding into complex cross-disciplinary play. Human-reviewed examples can establish standards without assuming reliable automated humour assessment.

For each content record, store the target word, clue, stated discipline, intended reasoning, accepted alternatives, ambiguity notes, partial-credit examples, exceptional-wit benchmarks with reasons, zero-credit examples, explanatory reveal, optional connections, factual sources where needed and review status.

Accept a round only when the target is defensible, required knowledge is signposted and alternatives have been reviewed. Verify that wrong answers cannot reach maximum through stacking and that exact answers need no unsolicited essay. For a future playable build, test the complete loop with keyboard and VoiceOver: hear the clue, submit, hear the award and reason, revisit feedback and continue.

Open decisions and guiding test

Resolve numerical weights, synonym acceptance, spelling and dictation handling, and disputed-award review before final scoring implementation. Decide how optional reasoning is entered and what distinguishes weak from substantive partial credit.

Hints, timers, multiplayer and automated judging remain possibilities, not commitments. Do not assume an AI judge can reliably or impartially recognise wit. A small reviewed content set can clarify the intended standard while larger decisions remain open.

The guiding test is simple: does this make the word more alive, reward genuine thought fairly and keep exact knowledge worth pursuing?
