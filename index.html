Ahsan’s apps

This is a list of your apps, each with a permanent GitHub Pages link. Your older links will keep working. Last updated 26 November 2026, Europe/London.

Sound sanctuaries

  • Ultimate Sanctuary, version 1. One app with six sound worlds: Clock and Fire, London, Countryside, Ocean and Underwater, River and Water Cycle, and Primordial.
  • Cosmic Sanctuary, version 2. A journey through the universe in sound, made mostly from real NASA recordings and data, with the Cosmologist Library audiobook.
  • Night Fire Clock Ensemble. Your ten clocks in conversation, with fire, crickets, cicadas, a singing bowl and chimes.
  • Clock Sanctuary. A clock-themed sanctuary with ticking clocks, chimes, binaural and isochronic tones, and a journey mode, all in one file.
  • Sound Sanctuary. The original layered sanctuary, with rain, stream, ocean, wind, birds, crickets, fire, underwater sounds, a train, a singing bowl, dolphins, a boat and brainwave tones.
  • Sound Sanctuary Night Fire. The earlier fire sanctuary, with campfire, crickets, cicadas, wind and tones.
  • City Sanctuary: London. A London soundscape, from the Tube and the Thames to cafes and parks, with occasional Big Ben, buses and pigeons.
  • Cityland Drift. A longer journey that drifts from London through the coast, a river and the countryside and back again, with seasons and travel sounds.
  • Water Cycle Sanctuary, version 4. Water, cave, rain, wind, leaves, hail and birds, with beat drift, a slowly gliding tone, an optional wake-up session and journeys of up to eight hours.

Learning and audiobooks

  • The Lost Library: Computer Science by Ear (newest). A hands-free spoken computer science course, from bits and logic gates to Python, algorithms, networks, security, AI and P versus NP, lectured by imagined versions of Newton, Leibniz, Boole, Faraday, Babbage, Lovelace, Hopper, Maxwell and Turing. About four and three quarter hours in 51 chapters, with an Audiobook mode and a Fast learning mode with spoken questions and an adjustable thinking pause.
  • Ice Age by Ear. A hands-free spoken lecture series on the Ice Age, from the coldest days to the first farmers, told by Demarchos of Massalia, an invented Greek who sailed with Pytheas. Europe first, then Siberia, the Americas, Australia, Africa, the Near East and East Asia. About four and three quarter hours in 25 chapters, with Book order, Explore shuffle, a sleep timer and quiet synthesised sounds.
  • The Last Light: Nuclear Apocalypse by Ear. A self-narrating audiobook about the bomb and the books that tried to warn the world, read by Colonel Andrew Fordyce of Edinburgh, 1750, with the Reader and Work Mate’s commentary. About two hours, with optional ambient sounds and the full text.
  • Maths by Ear, version 4 (newest, shorter pauses). The newest Maths by Ear, with shorter pauses in Professor Pennyfeather’s lectures and quizzes.
  • Maths by Ear, version 3. Professor Pennyfeather’s spoken maths lectures, with bracket tones and a tone tutorial.
  • Maths by Ear, version 2. Professor Pennyfeather's spoken maths lectures, the version before the shorter-pause and tone-tutorial updates.
  • Maths by Ear, version 1. The first Maths by Ear app: spoken maths lectures and readings.
  • History by Ear. A spoken history of mankind, narrated by Dr Edmund Thornleigh.
  • Night Library, version 2. Night Library version 2: a documentary audiobook of openly licensed articles, read aloud by the app.
  • Night Library, version 1. The first Night Library: a documentary audiobook of openly licensed articles, read aloud by the app.
  • Talking World Atlas. A talking map of countries, cities and landmarks that you can explore by touch, search and nearby places.
  • Road Crossing Trainer. An audio practice tool for listening to traffic and crossing a road in a safe, simulated setting. It is practice only, not a substitute for real-world training.
  • Body Care Companion. A VoiceOver-first body map, a short morning check, research reports, a glossary, quizzes, a timer and a breathing pacer. It is not a diagnosis.

Games

  • F1 by Ear. A Formula One race against rival cars, played by sound. Steer by turning the phone like a wheel, with pedals on the screen or automatic speed, a practice lesson and free practice.
  • F1 by Ear 2. The new version: a spoken race engineer who calls every corner, an eight-gear engine you can hear, and 100 tracks in 10 championships with points and standings.
  • Felt by Ear, version 7. Audio Texas Hold'em poker you can play by voice, with advice, odds, undo and emergency codes.
  • Tennis by Ear. An audio tennis game you can play by sound and, if you wish, by moving with the phone.
  • Golf by Ear: Motion Edition. Audio golf you play by swinging the phone, including aiming by bearing, a walk to the ball, and putts.
  • Golf by Ear. The earlier audio golf game, played with buttons or a simpler motion swing.
  • Desert Tanks: North Africa, 1940 to 1943. An audio tank battle set in the North African campaign, fought by bearing, sound and reports.
  • Desert Tanks: Motion Edition. The same North Africa battle, with walking, jab-to-fire and heading taken from the phone's motion sensors.
  • Battle of the Atlantic: destroyer and U-boat. An audio game in which you hunt a U-boat from a destroyer, or fight as the submarine, using hydrophone and engine sound.
  • Gravity Well. A short tilt-to-aim shooting game in which your shots bend around a gravity well.

Chess

  • Chess Browser, narrator version. A VoiceOver-first chess coach with a playable board, historic games, games from visually impaired Olympiads, spoken narration and piece sounds.
  • Chess Tutor. A simpler offline chess opponent at five strengths. The tutor gives advice but never moves for you, and you can walk the board with motion if you wish.

Other

  • Narration App. Paste or type any text and hear it read aloud smoothly, a sentence at a time, with pause, resume, sentence skipping, and voice and speed controls.
  • Desert Victory Described. Spoken description for the 1943 documentary Desert Victory, timed to play alongside the film.
  • Google Suite Accessibility Guide. A detailed guide to using Google apps accessibly with VoiceOver, and to moving from an iPhone to a Pixel with TalkBack.
